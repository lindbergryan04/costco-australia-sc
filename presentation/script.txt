SLIDE 2 — Why this matters                               ~30 sec
================================================================
TEACHES:  The ACCC is Australia's competition regulator;
          fuel prices are politically charged; big-box
          discounter entry is one of the cleanest natural
          experiments for testing whether new entrants
          discipline incumbent prices.

              7-Eleven, EG Group, Chevron/Caltex,
              United Petroleum, On The Run
            * Smaller independents (30+ brands):       26%
              Liberty, Metro Petroleum, Speedway,
              X-Convenience, Puma, COSTCO (highlighted blue),

NOTES:    Stylized facts here, not analysis. Keep the slide

SLIDE 3 — The question                                   ~25 sec
================================================================
TEACHES:  The precise causal question: does Costco
          gas-station entry into a local AU market cause
          nearby competitor stations to lower their prices?

VISUAL:   [BUILT] presentation/plots/03_market_unit.png
          Minimal conceptual schematic: a Costco dot at the
          center, ~10 competitor dots scattered inside a
          5 km coral ring, a few faint gray "outside the
          market" dots at the edges. Sits beside the
          question text on the slide and answers "what is
          the unit of analysis?" without restating the
          question.

NOTES:    Read the question once, slowly. Note that
          "competitor stations" is the dependent variable —
          we're NOT measuring whether Costco itself is
          cheap (it is, by design), we're measuring whether
          its arrival forces everyone else nearby to drop
          their prices too.

SLIDE 8 — Identification strategy                        ~40 sec
================================================================
TEACHES:  Synthetic control in plain English. For each
          treated Costco, we build a fake "Perth Airport
          that never got a Costco" as a weighted blend of
          WA donor postcodes whose pre-Costco price
          trajectory matches Perth Airport's. After Costco
          opens, the gap between the real and the synthetic
          is the causal effect — under the assumption that
          nothing else changed differently between Perth
          Airport and its synthetic.

VISUAL:   [BUILT] presentation/plots/08_costcos_rings_donors.png
          AU map showing 4 treated Costcos (blue dots with
          labels), the 5 km treated ring (coral, visible in
          the Coomera inset), the 20 km exclusion ring
          (dashed gray), and the 196 donor postcodes
          (green). Inset zooms into Coomera so the rings
          are legible.

NOTES:    The slide is the explanation; use the inset to
          make the geometry concrete. Allude to the method
          ("synthetic control") in one breath; do NOT
          explain the optimization. The professor explicitly
          warns against "drowning in technical details."

================================================================
SLIDE 9 — Headline result: Perth Airport                 ~50 sec
================================================================
TEACHES:  The picture that, if a viewer remembered nothing
          else, would convey our main finding. Before Feb
          2020, synthetic and actual track each other
          tightly — proof the counterfactual is credible.
          After Costco opens, actual drops ~3.4 c/L below
          synthetic and STAYS there for 6 years. The 95%
          CI on the mean effect excludes zero; the MSPE
          ratio (post-period gap / pre-period noise) is
          4.31, well above the standard credibility bar.

VISUAL:   [BUILT] presentation/plots/09_perth_airport_trajectories.png
          Actual (solid blue) vs synthetic (dashed) with
          donor-permutation 95% CI band, vertical dotted
          line at Feb 2020 (Costco opens), annotation box
          with mean gap and MSPE ratio.

NOTES:    Walk the audience through the axes BEFORE the
          finding: x = months from 2018 to 2026, y = mean
          unleaded price within 5 km of Perth Airport, in
          cents/L. Then point at the pre-period tracking,
          THEN the treatment line, THEN the divergence.
          This is the longest single slide — earn it by
          slowing down.

================================================================
SLIDE 10 — Does it generalize?                           ~45 sec
================================================================
TEACHES:  Honest read across all 4 treated Costcos. Only
          Perth Airport produces a clean negative effect
          with a CI that excludes zero. Casuarina is
          borderline (degenerate-narrow CI is a known
          artifact of having few stations in the ring).
          Coomera and Lake Macquarie are inconclusive —
          Lake Macquarie is actually wrong-signed, but
          that's traceable to a 5 km geometry artifact
          (see RC7 in the qmd).

VISUAL:   [BUILT] presentation/plots/10_forest_plot.png
          Forest plot with one row per Costco: point
          estimate as a colored dot, 95% CI as a horizontal
          bar, zero line marked vertically, dots colored by
          sign of effect.

NOTES:    Resist any temptation to spin. The professor
          explicitly penalizes overselling weak results.
          The honest "1 of 4 clean" framing makes the Perth
          Airport effect MORE credible, not less, because
          we're not cherry-picking.

================================================================
SLIDE 11 — Did we just get lucky?                        ~35 sec
================================================================
TEACHES:  One robustness check shown in full + a quick
          name-check of the other six we ran. Headline
          check: placebo-in-time. We pretend Costco arrived
          12 months EARLY at Perth Airport, refit the
          synthetic to the truncated pre-period, and check
          whether a fake "effect" appears at the fake date.
          It doesn't — the trajectories cross the fake date
          smoothly and only diverge at the real one.

          entry. And treat the Costco price-discipline
          effect as a substantive consideration in any
          fuel-retail merger review.

================================================================
SLIDE 13 — Limitations                                   ~25 sec
================================================================
TEACHES:  What we CAN'T claim from this data:
            1. Only one truly clean case (Perth Airport).
               Generalizing to "Costco always disciplines"
               is overreach.
            2. The 5 km ring is a researcher choice;
               results at 3 km and 8 km differ somewhat.
            3. We identify the EFFECT, not the MECHANISM.
               Is it price-matching? Volume discounts?
               Consumer perception? Our data can't tell.
            4. QLD and NSW Costcos need more post-treatment
               months before a clean read is possible.

VISUAL:   [TEXT] Clean text list, one limitation per row,
          small icon (warning triangle / map pin / question
          mark / clock) next to each for visual hierarchy.

NOTES:    Don't dwell — 25 seconds tops. The honest framing
          here protects against the "overselling" deduction.
          Close with a one-line bridge to the closing slide
          or "thank you, questions?"
