COSTCO AUSTRALIA  ·  SHREW REVIEW FILM FESTIVAL
Presentation blueprint (the WHY behind every slide)

This document is the design rationale and delivery guidance for
each slide. The spoken words live in script.txt. Use this file
when deciding what a slide is FOR and how to deliver it; use

Target runtime: ~7:00 of content (~25 sec slack)
Required structure per assignment PDF §3.1 – §3.9

What we are optimizing for (per assignment §5):
  - Narrative arc: problem  ->  finding  ->  recommendation
  - Main finding front and center, not buried
  - Every minute earned (no filler, no throat-clearing)
  - Selective: a few great figures, not every chart we made
  - Every figure walked through (axes, pattern, meaning)
  - Recommendation grounded in data + back-of-envelope
  - Every group member has a speaking role
  - Polished visuals (no raw output, no pie charts)
  - Honest about weak results (no overselling)

SLIDE 2  ·  Why this matters                            ~30 sec
================================================================
TEACHES:  Three things in one slide:
            1. The ACCC is Australia's competition regulator.
            2. The AU fuel-retail market is concentrated
               (74% of volume in 9 brands).
            3. Big-box entry is one of the cleanest natural
               experiments for testing whether new entrants
               discipline incumbent prices.

VISUAL:   [BUILT] 02_market_concentration.png
          Three horizontal bars (Major / Other large /
          Smaller independents) showing the ACCC's category
          aggregates from its Nov 2025 report. Costco is
          highlighted in blue inside the smaller-independents
          bar. Callout under the bars carries the takeaway.

NOTES:    Stylized facts only, not analysis. Keep the slide
          uncluttered. End with the bridge: "...which raises
          a sharp empirical question:" so the handoff to
          slide 3 feels inevitable.

SPEAKER:  Speaker 1


================================================================
SLIDE 3  ·  The question                                ~25 sec
================================================================
TEACHES:  The precise causal question and the unit of
          analysis. Does Costco entry into an AU local market
          (a 5 km ring) cause nearby competitor stations to
          lower their prices?

VISUAL:   [BUILT] 03_market_unit.png
          Conceptual schematic: a single Costco dot in the
          middle, ~10 competitor stations inside a coral 5 km
          ring, faint gray "outside the market" dots beyond.
          Answers "what is the unit of analysis?" without
          restating the question.

NOTES:    Read the question once, slowly. Make the point that
          "competitor stations" is the dependent variable; we
          are NOT measuring whether Costco itself is cheap
          (it is, by design). We are measuring whether its
          arrival forces everyone else nearby to drop prices.

SLIDE 7  ·  Sample construction                         ~30 sec
================================================================
TEACHES:  How we went from the full universe to the analysis
          sample. Two filters per side:
            Costcos:  10 candidates -> 4 with enough pre AND
                      post coverage.
            Postcodes: 1,004 -> 196 donors (>20 km from any
                      Costco, ≥3 stations/month average).

VISUAL:   [BUILT] 07_sample_funnel.png
          Two side-by-side funnels (Treated Costcos on left,
          Donor postcodes on right). Bar widths proportional
          to counts; final layers in saturated color (deep
          blue for treated, moss green for donors). Filter
          reasons sit between layers with arrows.

NOTES:    Do NOT walk through every filter from the qmd. Hit
          the two big ones per side. The audience should come
          away thinking "principled, not arbitrary." Six
          excluded Costcos (Marsden Park, Auburn, Canberra
          Airport, Casula, North Lakes, Ipswich) are too
          recent or too old. Name them only if asked.

SPEAKER:  Speaker 2

SLIDE 8  ·  Identification strategy                     ~40 sec
================================================================
TEACHES:  Synthetic control in plain English. For each
          treated Costco, we build a fake "Perth Airport that
          never got a Costco" as a weighted blend of WA donor
          postcodes whose pre-Costco trajectory matches Perth
          Airport's. After Costco opens, the gap is the
          causal effect, under the assumption that nothing
          else changed differently between the treated ring
          and its synthetic.

VISUAL:   [BUILT] 08_costcos_rings_donors.png
          AU map showing the 4 treated Costcos (blue dots),
          the 20 km exclusion ring (dashed gray) around each,
          and the 196 donor postcodes (green). Inset zooms
          into Coomera so the 5 km treated ring (coral) is
          legible.

NOTES:    Allude to the method ("synthetic control") in one
          breath; do NOT explain the optimization. The PDF
          (§6 deductions) explicitly warns against drowning
          in technical details. The map IS the explanation.

SLIDE 10  ·  Does it generalize?                        ~45 sec
================================================================
TEACHES:  Honest read across all 4 treated Costcos: only
          Perth Airport produces a clean negative effect with
          a CI that excludes zero. Casuarina is borderline
          (degenerate-narrow CI artifact). Coomera is
          inconclusive. Lake Macquarie is wrong-signed, but
          that traces to a 5 km geometry artifact (RC7).

VISUAL:   [BUILT] 10_forest_plot.png
          Forest plot with one row per Costco: point estimate
          as a colored dot, 95% CI as a horizontal bar, zero
          line marked vertically. Dots colored by sign-of-
          effect class.

NOTES:    Resist any temptation to spin. The PDF (§6) and
          the plan-of-attack both penalize overselling weak
          results. The honest "1 of 4 clean" framing makes
          the Perth Airport effect MORE credible, not less,
          because we are not cherry-picking.

SLIDE 11  ·  Did we just get lucky?                     ~35 sec
================================================================
TEACHES:  Headline robustness check (placebo-in-time) shown
          in full plus a quick name-check of the other six.
          Honest read: the Perth Airport placebo at Feb 2019
          shows a gap of -1.96 c/L with CI excluding zero,
          meaning some pre-existing WA price drift. But the
          real post-Costco gap (-3.4) is meaningfully larger,
          so the Costco-incremental effect is ~-1.4 c/L.
          Still real, just smaller than the headline.

VISUAL:   [BUILT] 11_placebo_robustness.png
          Top panel: placebo trajectory for Perth Airport
          (Jan 2018 – Feb 2020), with vertical lines at the
          placebo date (solid dotted) and real date (faint).
          Bottom-right callout stacks three numbers in
          monospace: placebo gap + CI, real gap, Costco-
          incremental.
          Bottom strip: a 3-row Q/A table. Each row is a
          concern someone might raise (italic, left) paired
          with the check we ran and its finding (green check
          + RC label + plain text, right). Per PDF §3.7 we
          show only the most important / most intuitive
          checks (RC4 drop COVID, RC2 alt radii, RC7 spatial
          placebo); the strip header acknowledges "we ran
          seven total" and points to the analysis document
          for the rest (RC3, RC5, RC6).

NOTES:    The point of this slide is NOT the methodology, it
          is the CREDIBILITY STATEMENT: "We tried to break
          our own finding seven different ways; here is the
          most intuitive one, and the rest are in our
          analysis document." Be honest about the placebo
          imperfection. The qmd at line 1837 acknowledges
          the same caveat. Honesty here protects against the
          overselling deduction.

SLIDE 12  ·  Recommendation + back-of-envelope          ~35 sec
================================================================
TEACHES:  Concrete action for the ACCC plus a back-of-
          envelope on the consumer impact.

          Two recommendations:
            1. Use advocacy + merger-review powers to lower
               zoning and lease barriers that delay big-box
               fuel entry.
            2. Treat the Costco price-discipline effect as
               a substantive consideration in any future
               fuel-retail merger review.

          Back-of-envelope (from qmd §5.3, Scenario A):
            assume 100,000 residents per 5 km ring
            assume 900 L/person/year petrol consumption (ABS)
            assume 70% of ring volume goes to non-Costco
                   competitors (1 Costco vs ~20 incumbents)
            =>  ~63 ML/year competitor fuel per ring
          Costco-specific effect: ~1.4 ¢/L
            =>  ~A$0.9 million in savings per ring per year
            =>  ~A$3.6 million across the 4 current rings
            =>  ~A$9 million at 10 rings if barriers fall

VISUAL:   [BUILT] 12_recommendation.png
          Magazine-style two-column layout, thin vertical rule
          between halves. Left: kicker "RECOMMENDATIONS FOR
          THE ACCC", then two numbered recommendations with
          bold headlines and supporting prose. Right: kicker
          "BACK-OF-ENVELOPE IMPACT", per-ring math chain
          (63 ML × 1.4 ¢/L = A$0.9 M as three stat blocks
          with × and = operators), a horizontal rule, the
          scale-up rationale, and a dominant green hero
          "≈ A$9 M / year" with "aggregate annual consumer
          savings" subtitle.

NOTES:    Be SPECIFIC. Vague recommendations get marked down
          (PDF §6). Read the math out loud; it lands harder
          when the audience sees the arithmetic assemble in
          front of them. Anchor on Scenario A (Costco-
          specific 1.4 c/L), not Scenario B (the 4-Costco
          mean, dragged toward zero by Lake Macquarie and
          Coomera). Naming the assumptions out loud also
          inoculates against "where did you get that?"

SPEAKER:  Speaker 4


================================================================
SLIDE 13  ·  Limitations                                ~25 sec
================================================================
TEACHES:  The four biggest threats to our conclusion that
          our robustness checks did not fully resolve:
            1. Only one truly clean case (Perth Airport).
               Generalizing to "Costco always disciplines"
               is overreach.
            2. The 5 km ring is a researcher choice; results
               at 3 km and 8 km differ.
            3. We identify the EFFECT, not the MECHANISM.
               Price-matching? Volume discounts? Perception?
               Our data cannot tell.
            4. QLD and NSW Costcos need more post-treatment
               months before a clean read is possible.

VISUAL:   [BUILT] 13_limitations.png
          Four stacked rows separated by thin horizontal
          rules. Each row has a numbered colored badge on the
          left (orange / blue / purple / gray), a kicker label
          naming the kind of concern (GENERALIZABILITY /
          GEOMETRY / MECHANISM / DATA WINDOW), a bold
          headline, and a one-line body. Color-coded so the
          audience can scan the four concern-types visually.

NOTES:    Do not dwell — 25 seconds tops. The honest framing
          here protects against the overselling deduction.
          Close with "thank you" and a clean handoff to Q&A
          (if any) or end-card.

SPEAKER:  Speaker 4
